I’m Shaina, a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the University of Bonn and a research assistant in the CAISA Lab. My expertise is in large language models, generative AI and model training.

My background spans software engineering, university teaching and NLP research. I bring those perspectives together to build language technologies, investigate how information is framed, and evaluate what models can reliably do.

I work across the research pipeline: dataset and annotation design, coding, model training, evaluation and supervision. My research focuses on disinformation, multilingual NLP, and the linguistic structures behind persuasive and ideological messages.

Selected projects

The problems, methods and applied relevance behind my collaborative research.

01 / Retrieval & LLMsSemEval 2025

Finding fact-checked claims
across languages.

Matching a new claim to an existing fact-check is a semantic search problem. This project combines embedding-based retrieval with LLaMA relevance assessment to rank candidate claims across languages.

  • Multilingual embeddings
  • Semantic search
  • LLM reranking
Inside the project +

Approach

Compare Jina, E5 and MPNet embeddings, retrieve candidate claims with cosine similarity, then use structured prompts for LLaMA relevance scoring.

Applied relevance

Retrieval pipelines, ranking evaluation and choosing embedding models for multilingual search.

Coauthor · CAISA at SemEval-2025 Task 7

Read the paper ↗
02 / Training & transferAbjadNLP 2025

MultiProp:
learning across languages.

Propaganda detection has to work beyond languages with abundant labels. MultiProp explores transformer ensembles, translation-based augmentation, meta-learning and text chunking for multilingual classification.

  • Transformer ensembles
  • Meta-learning
  • Long-text processing
Inside the project +

Approach

Combine encoder, decoder and hybrid model families; study few-shot transfer and split long documents into manageable segments.

Applied relevance

Adapting classifiers to limited training data, new languages and inputs longer than a model’s context window.

Coauthor · MultiProp Framework

Read the paper ↗
03 / Data & modellingLREC-COLING 2024

DeFaktS:
beyond a single label.

Disinformation involves more than whether a post is true or false. DeFaktS introduces a German social-media dataset and a fine-grained annotation framework for analysing how misleading content is framed.

  • Dataset design
  • Fine-grained classification
  • Transformer baselines
Inside the project +

Approach

Define annotation categories for textual signals and compare classical machine-learning approaches with transformer-based classifiers, including German BERT.

Applied relevance

Task definition, labelled-data development and establishing baselines for specialised NLP problems.

First author · DeFaktS

Read the paper ↗
04 / Representation learningWOAH 2024

Understanding information
through user context.

The same text can mean different things in different contexts. This work studies user representations for classifying unreliable posts, identifying spreaders and predicting future sharing behaviour.

  • User embeddings
  • Personalisation
  • Temporal prediction
Inside the project +

Approach

Compare User2Vec, task-trained user embeddings and representations derived from authorship attribution using social-media histories.

Applied relevance

Context-aware classification, representation learning and evaluating predictions over time.

First author · Harnessing Personalization Methods
